\begin{abstract}
We give an explicit real Hadamard matrix of order $7572=12\cdot631$.
The construction starts with four periodic T-sequences of length $631$,
specified by 31 signed orbit states. They give a compatible sign frame for
a rational action of $\Q(\zeta_6)$, and a complete $BH(3,6)$ source then
produces the claimed matrix. The source Gram scale is $3$, the frame scale
is $2524$, and their product is the endpoint order. All sequence
correlations and all row inner products of the order-$7572$ matrix have been verified by exact
integer calculations, including an independent check of the saved matrix.
The same frame gives orders $17668$, $22716$, $32812$, $98436$ and
$295308$. A further restriction from products of cyclotomic fields gives
eight additional new orders and explicit infinite families. Their weighted
sources are complete orthogonal designs; their restricted entries are signs.
Four first expanded family members give further new orders, bringing the
total to eighteen, all new to the best of the author's knowledge.
The method also gives new periodic circulant T-matrices of order $127$,
listed as the smallest open T-matrix order in the 2026 survey.
Our literature comparison is dated 24 November 2026.
\end{abstract}

\begin{theorem}
There exists a matrix $H\in\{-1,1\}^{7572\times7572}$ such that
$HH^{\mathsf T}=7572I$.
\end{theorem}

The scalar-transfer framework is classical; see, for example, Egan and
\'{O}~Cath\'ain~\cite{morphisms} and Compton, Craigen and de~Launey~\cite{unreal}.
The twisted length-$631$ search chart is
recorded in the existing search notes~\cite{frontier}. The contribution here
is the completed integer witness and its verified flat scalar frame.

\paragraph{The finite witness.}
Put $p=631$ and $\mu=458\in\mathbb Z_p^*$, of order $21$.
Let $C$ fix channel $0$ and cyclically permute channels $1,2,3$.
Enumerate the origin first, followed by the nonzero multiplication-by-$\mu$
orbits, taking the least unused residue as the next representative.
The states, in that order, are
\begin{center}\small\ttfamily
0 0 5 4 4 0 7 0 7 2 3 0 1 2 6 1\\
4 2 0 1 1 4 3 4 7 3 3 5 6 2 7
\end{center}
A state $2j+\varepsilon$ at representative $r$ means
$b(\mu^k r)=(-1)^\varepsilon C^k e_j$, where $e_j$ is the $j$th standard
basis vector of $\mathbb Z^4$; the first state gives $b(0)=e_0$.
This specifies all four sequences $b_0,\ldots,b_3$ without a search.
They have disjoint support, exactly one signed nonzero entry at each
position, and row sums $(22,7,7,7)$.
A direct integer calculation from these states gives all $631$ identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T}
 \sum_{i=0}^3\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z_p} b_i(x)b_i(x+s)
       =p\,\delta_{s,0}\qquad(s\in\mathbb Z_p).
\end{equation}
The complete sequences and a standalone replay of this finite calculation
are supplied with the matrix. These are periodic T-sequences; no
aperiodic correlation assertion is required.

\paragraph{Sign frames.}
Let
\[
 D=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&1&1&1\\-1&0&1&-1\\-1&-1&0&1\\-1&1&-1&0
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad a(x)=Db(x).
\]
Then $D^{\mathsf T}=-D$ and $D^{\mathsf T}D=3I$.
Consequently the summed periodic autocorrelation of $a$ is
$3p\delta_{s,0}$ and the summed symmetric mixed correlation of $a,b$
vanishes. Their supports complement each other at every entry, and each
nonzero entry is a sign.

Here is the precise Goethals--Seidel convention used. For four sequences
$f_i$, let $X_i$ be the circulant with $(X_i)_{uv}=f_i(v-u)$,
and let $J_{uv}=1$ when $u+v=p-1$, with zero entries otherwise. Define
\[
\GS(f)=\begin{pmatrix}
X_0&X_1J&X_2J&X_3J\\
-X_1J&X_0&-X_3^{\mathsf T}J&X_2^{\mathsf T}J\\
-X_2J&X_3^{\mathsf T}J&X_0&-X_1^{\mathsf T}J\\
-X_3J&-X_2^{\mathsf T}J&X_1^{\mathsf T}J&X_0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Put $U=\GS(a)$, $V=\GS(b)$ and $d=4p=2524$.
The circulant and reversal identities, together with \eqref{eq:T}, give
\[
 UU^{\mathsf T}=3pI,\quad VV^{\mathsf T}=pI,\quad
 UV^{\mathsf T}+VU^{\mathsf T}=0.
\]
Thus the literal sign matrices $P=U-V$ and $Q=U+V$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frame}
 PP^{\mathsf T}=QQ^{\mathsf T}=dI,\qquad
 PQ^{\mathsf T}+QP^{\mathsf T}=dI.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Scalar action and transported metric.}
Set $R=QP^{\mathsf T}/d$. Equation~\eqref{eq:frame} implies
\[
 RR^{\mathsf T}=I,\qquad R^{\mathsf T}=I-R,\qquad
 R^2-R+I=0,\qquad RP=Q.
\]
Hence $\rho(\zeta_6)=R$ defines a faithful $\Q(\zeta_6)$ action on
$\Q^d$, with $\rho(\overline z)=\rho(z)^{\mathsf T}$.
The field degree is $2$ and the module rank is $1262$; the rational
dimension is $2524$.
Its rational entry alphabet is specified exactly by
$R_{ij}=(d-2\operatorname{Ham}(Q_i,P_j))/d$ and recorded in the certificate.
To specify the incoming metric explicitly, put $S=2R-I$ and
$\xi=\sqrt{-3}$. The Hermitian form, linear in its second argument,
\[
 h(u,v)=\frac{u^{\mathsf T}v}{2}
         -\frac{u^{\mathsf T}Sv}{6}\,\xi
\]
satisfies $\Tr_{\Q(\zeta_6)/\Q}h(u,v)=u^{\mathsf T}v$.
Indeed $S^{\mathsf T}=-S$ and $S^2=-3I$.
The canonical field basis $(1,\zeta_6)$ has trace metric
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$;
the displayed $h$ specifies its realization in the actual coordinates.

Take the complete Butson source
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}-1&\zeta_6&\zeta_6\\
 \zeta_6&-1&\zeta_6\\\zeta_6&\zeta_6&-1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad MM^*=3I_3.
\]
Its used alphabet is exactly $\{-1,\zeta_6\}$.
Restriction through $\rho$ preserves the Gram scale $3$ in the
transported metric; the intermediate blocks may be nonflat.
The frame $F=P$ has $FF^{\mathsf T}=dI$, and the used symbols have flat
images $-F=-P$ and $\rho(\zeta_6)F=Q$.
Therefore
\[
 H=\rho(M)\operatorname{diag}(P,P,P)
   =\begin{pmatrix}-P&Q&Q\\Q&-P&Q\\Q&Q&-P\end{pmatrix}
   \in\{-1,1\}^{3d\times3d},\qquad
 HH^{\mathsf T}=3dI=7572I.
\]
This proves the theorem. The source scale $3$, frame scale $2524$ and
expanded order $7572$ are distinct quantities until the final calculation.
No flatness assertion is needed for unused roots: for example
$\rho(\zeta_6^2)F=Q-P=2V$ has zeros and entries of magnitude two.

\paragraph{Further completed scalar endpoints.}
The same action and frame admit the four-symbol alphabet
$\mathcal A=\{1,\zeta_6,-1,-\zeta_6\}$, with images $P,Q,-P,-Q$.
Consequently, for any complete $N\in\mathcal A^{n\times n}$ with
$NN^*=nI$, the literal blocks $\rho(N_{ij})P$ form a real Hadamard
matrix of order $2524n$. Indeed, their $(i,j)$ Gram block is exactly
$2524\rho(\sum_kN_{ik}\overline{N_{jk}})=2524n\delta_{ij}I$.
The retained gauges of the published sources $P_7$, the first $BH(9,6)$
catalogue representative, and $M_{13}A$~\cite{catalogue,aalto}, followed
by the triplings below, give the following completed constructions.
Here ``known'' means that an earlier construction was located; ``new''
means new to the best of the author's knowledge.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccl}
Source order/scale & Endpoint order/scale & Order status\\\hline
$3$ & $7572$ & New\\
$7$ & $17668$ & New\\
$9$ & $22716$ & New\\
$13$ & $32812$ & New\\
$39$ & $98436$ & New\\
$117$ & $295308$ & New\\
$351$ & $885924$ & Known (Paley)
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Every used exponent is in $\{0,1,3,4\}$, and both complete source Grams
are checked over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_6)$. The ancillary files specify the
gauged sources and every endpoint entry, without storing the large matrices.

The known unreal-source tripling rule~\cite{unreal} extends the order-$13$ source.
Write an unreal source as $(A+\sqrt{-3}B)/2$, with $A,B$ sign matrices.
For $C=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
put $A'=B\otimes J_3$ and $B'=A\otimes I_3+B\otimes C$.
The identities $CC^{\mathsf T}=3I-J_3$ and $CJ_3=0$ verify the
order-$3n$ unreal source. Multiplication by $\zeta_6^{-1}$ gives palette
$\mathcal A$ again. Starting at order $13$ yields real orders
$32812\cdot3^k$ for every $k\geq0$; the cases $k=1,2$ give
$98436$ and $295308$ and have also been checked as complete finite sources.
The next case, $885924$ ($k=3$), is known by Paley's construction since
$885923$ is prime.

\paragraph{Further cyclotomic scalar restrictions.}
The same periodic T631 witness yields four disjoint coefficient matrices
$X_0,\ldots,X_3$ of order $4p$, with $p=631$, by applying $\GS$ to
\[
b,\quad(-b_1,b_0,b_3,-b_2),\quad
(-b_2,-b_3,b_0,b_1),\quad(-b_3,b_2,-b_1,b_0).
\]
The T identities and commuting-circulant GS identity give
$X_jX_j^{\mathsf T}=pI$ and
$X_iX_j^{\mathsf T}+X_jX_i^{\mathsf T}=0$ for $i\ne j$.
Their supports partition every matrix position. Thus
$D(x)=\sum_jX_jx_j$ is a complete orthogonal design with Gram
$p\sum_j x_j^2I$ for commuting self-adjoint variables.

Take a verified symmetric circulant Williamson quadruple $A_j$ of order
$w$, with first rows $a_j$ and $\sum_j A_j^2=4wI$.
In $K_w=\Q[c]/(c^w-1)$ put
$f_j=\sum_k a_j[k]c^k$ and $\bar c=c^{-1}$.
Then $\bar f_j=f_j$ and $\sum_jf_j^2=4w$.
The weighted source $D(f)$ has order $4p$, alphabet the used polynomials
$\pm f_j$, and Gram scale $4pw$; it is not asserted to be Butson.
The algebra is the product $\prod_{d\mid w}\Q(\zeta_d)$, with positive
trace functional $\tau(a)=[c^0]a=w^{-1}\sum_{d\mid w}\Tr(a_d)$.
Its metric in the basis $\beta_k=c^{-k}$ is exactly $I_w$.
Multiplication gives $\rho(f_j)=A_j$, with conjugation transported by
transpose. Restriction, with terminal frame $I_w$, therefore gives
\[
 H_w=\sum_{j=0}^3X_j\otimes A_j,\qquad
 H_wH_w^{\mathsf T}=4pwI.
\]
Each block position selects one signed $A_j$, proving all entries are signs.
This recovers the Cooper--Wallis construction~\cite{cooperwallis} as a
weighted scalar restriction. The ancillary table supplies 27 literal
quadruples from the pinned Sage implementation~\cite{sage}; every complete
source Gram and amicability identity is checked. Eight further orders are new:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rr@{\qquad}rr}
$w$ & $4pw$ & $w$ & $4pw$\\\hline
$17$ & $42908$ & $23$ & $58052$\\
$29$ & $73196$ & $31$ & $78244$\\
$37$ & $93388$ & $43$ & $108532$\\
$51$ & $128724$ & $61$ & $153964$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The other inputs are marked known when a prior construction was located;
in particular $w=33$ gives the known Paley block-product order $83292$.

\paragraph{Constructive infinite families.}
Spence's recurrence~\cite{spence} applies to suitable sign quadruples
$(D,E,F,G)$ of order $(q+1)/2$, for a prime power $q\equiv1\pmod4$.
Let $Q$ be the symmetric Paley matrix of order $q$, so
$Q^2=qI-J$ and $QJ=0$. For each pair $(U,V)=(D,E),(F,G)$ set
\[
 U'=\tfrac12(U-V)\otimes J+\tfrac12(U+V)\otimes(Q+I),\quad
 V'=\tfrac12(U-V)\otimes J+\tfrac12(U+V)\otimes(Q-I).
\]
The retained starters satisfy
$M_0=(D-E)^2+(F-G)^2=4I$ and
$P_0=(D+E)^2+(F+G)^2=4qI$.
The recurrence gives $M'=P\otimes I$ and
$P'=qP\otimes I+(qM-P)\otimes J$; hence
$M_r=4q^rI$, $P_r=4q^{r+1}I$, and the four squared components sum to
$4w_rI$, where $w_r=(q+1)q^r/2$.
Symmetry and commutativity persist. Exactly one of $(u-v)/2,(u+v)/2$
is nonzero for paired signs, so every new component entry is a sign.

Explicit starters and entry recursions are supplied for
$q=13,17,61,101,121$. They are group-circulant on
$C_{(q+1)/2}\times(\F_q,+)^r$.
The same normalized group-algebra trace has metric $I_{w_r}$ in the
inverse-group basis, and the scalar proof above gives the five families
\[
17668\cdot13^r,\quad22716\cdot17^r,\quad78244\cdot61^r,\quad
128724\cdot101^r,\quad153964\cdot121^r\qquad(r\geq0).
\]
Every first expanded source is verified coefficientwise by an exact
integer transform with a proved coefficient bound. The ancillary recipes
give entry and row access without allocating the full endpoints.
Their first expanded orders $229684$, $386172$, $4772884$ and $13001124$
are new. The fifth, $18629644$, is known by Paley II with prime $9314821$.

\paragraph{How the witness was found.}
The successful search separated orbit axes from signs, using an auxiliary
finite field in characteristic two. This field is unrelated to the
characteristic-zero scalar action above.
Encode the four axis labels by $0,1,\omega,\omega^2\in\F_4$, so that
$c(\mu x)=\omega c(x)$. The parity of a signed correlation depends only
on these labels.
There is a bijection between all such axis words with the required
correlation parities and $\mathrm{SL}_2(\F_{2^{15}})$.
Here is a brief derivation.
Since $\operatorname{ord}_{631}(2)=45$ and
$\langle4,\mu\rangle$ is the quadratic-residue subgroup, the Fourier
coefficients have two nonzero-frequency orbits, based at $1$ and $-1$.
In $\F_{2^{90}}$, choose a primitive $631$st root $\zeta$ and an element
$t$ of order $27$, with $\omega=t^{18}$.
The Fourier rules $z_{4k}=z_k^4$ and $z_{\mu k}=\omega^{-1}z_k$,
with $4^{15}=\mu^{14}\pmod {631}$ and $t^{2^{30}}=\omega t$,
give $z_1=t\alpha$ and $z_{-1}=t\beta$, for
$\alpha,\beta\in\F_{2^{30}}$.
The $2^{45}$-Frobenius fixes $\zeta$ and sends $t$ to $t^{-1}$.
The indicator identity $[c=d]=1+(c+d)^3$ reduces the full parity condition
to $\overline\alpha\beta+\alpha\overline\beta=1$, where the bar is
the relative $2^{15}$-Frobenius; the zero-frequency condition is automatic.
Writing $\alpha=u+\omega v$ and $\beta=r+\omega s$ over
$K=\F_{2^{15}}$ gives just
\[
 us+vr=1,\qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}u&r\\v&s\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_2(K).
\]
The fixed inverse Fourier transform is an invertible binary map on
$60$ coordinates. Thus the parameter space has
$(2^{30}-1)2^{15}$ points, and uniform sampling requires no parity
rejection.

For a fixed axis word the exact correlation in shift class $\ell$ has
the form
\[
 C_\ell(x)=C_\ell(0)-2\sum_{i<j}W_{\ell,ij}(x_i\mathbin\oplus x_j),
\]
where $x_i$ is the orbit sign bit and $W_{\ell,ij}$ is an integer weight.
Extend $W$ symmetrically with zero diagonal.
Modulo four these are linear equations in the bits, with coefficient
$\sum_j W_{\ell,ij}$ at $x_i$ and right side $C_\ell(0)/2$ over $\F_2$.
On the resulting
affine kernel, $C_\ell/4$ modulo two is quadratic, allowing a packed
modulo-eight filter. Exact DC counts and all integer correlations are
tested on its survivors.
The winning single-thread stream, restricted to nonorigin head counts
$5,7,9$, found the stated head-count-$9$ witness after $2347.74$ seconds.
It sign-lifted $52\,029\,943$ sampled axis words; the sum of their
affine-kernel sizes was $426\,230\,308\,864$, processed by packed filters
until the first exact zero. It passed $5\,202\,822$ assignments through
the exact DC filter.
These are measurements of that stream, not a bound on rediscovery time.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:T127}
There exist four circulant $\{0,\pm1\}$-matrices $T_i$ of order $127$
with pairwise disjoint support and
$\sum_{i=0}^3T_iT_i^{\mathsf T}=127I$.
\end{theorem}
For a deterministic witness, use the same orbit convention with length
$127$, multiplier $22$ of order $9$, and the $15$ states
\begin{center}\small\ttfamily
0 0 4 0 1 6 2 3 6 1 0 3 5 4 3
\end{center}
The resulting one-hot sequences have row sums $(10,3,3,3)$ and weights
$(46,27,27,27)$. Direct integer calculation gives \eqref{eq:T} with
$p=127$, proving Theorem~\ref{thm:T127} by taking their circulants.
The independent verification checks every shift. The corresponding
scalar frame has rational dimension and Gram scale $508$, field degree
$2$, and module rank $254$. With the same trace metric, source and actual
used alphabet, it gives a fully checked $H1524$, an already known order.
The search used $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb F_{128})$ and found this witness
after $73$ axis lifts; all $407$ coefficients of the binary parity identity
were checked. These periodic T-matrices are new to the best of the author's
knowledge. Table~3 of the survey~\cite{survey} explicitly lists $127$ as
its smallest open T-matrix order.
Its complete data and separate archive-free verifier are included in the
ancillary subdirectory \texttt{anc/t127/}.

\paragraph{Exact verification and availability.}
The standalone Python/NumPy recipe reconstructs the matrix from the states,
checks every expanded correlation, the complete identities
\eqref{eq:frame}, and every endpoint row inner product.
A separate verifier read the actual packed matrix and checked all
$28\,663\,806$ distinct row pairs by integer bit counts; every Hamming
distance is $3786$. The packed rows have $7572$ used bits, little-endian
within each byte, and zero padding. Their SHA256 is
\begin{center}\scriptsize\ttfamily
bc3cb6fca9243a6ef01c82de2a446080c8866e0f30d76c0f23ef0508ae5e2e9b
\end{center}
The matrix, sequence data, recipe and independent audits are retained in
\texttt{artifacts/hadamard-7572/}.
All data needed to reconstruct $H7572$ are given above: the orbit
states and the literal block conventions determine every entry.
The primary survey's Table~5 lists $p=631$ in its unresolved $4\cdot3p$
row~\cite{survey}; the other two records explicitly list $7572$ as
unresolved~\cite{explorer,frontier}. For the remaining orders marked
new above, the literature search located no earlier construction.
